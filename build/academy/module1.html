Module 1: AI & Generative AI Foundations

📖 Lesson 1: What is Artificial Intelligence (AI)?

Definition

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the technology that enables computers and machines to think, learn, solve problems, and make decisions in a way that is similar to humans.

These tasks include:

  • Learning
  • Thinking
  • Understanding language
  • Solving problems
  • Making decisions
  • Recognizing images and voices

Simple Definition

AI is teaching computers to think and learn like humans.

💡 Easy Trick to Remember

AI = Artificial + Intelligence
Artificial = Man-made (created by humans)
Intelligence = Ability to learn, think, reason, and solve problems.

Real-Life Examples

Example AI Task
ChatGPT Answers questions
Siri Voice Assistant
Google Maps Finds shortest route
Netflix Movie Recommendations
Amazon Product Suggestions
Face Unlock Face Recognition
Gmail Spam Detection
Tesla Self Driving

How AI Works

Data
↓
Learning Algorithm
↓
AI Model
↓
Prediction / Answer

Example: Thousands of cat photos → AI learns cat features → Shows new image → AI says "It's a Cat"

Types of AI

1. Narrow AI (Weak AI)

Narrow AI is an AI system that is built to do one job very well, but it cannot do other tasks like a human.

Examples: ChatGPT, Google Translate, Alexa, Face Unlock

Simple Example: Think of a calculator. It can solve mathematical calculations very accurately. But it cannot write an essay, drive a car, or diagnose a disease. Similarly, Google Translate can translate languages but cannot independently manage a business or cook food. This is Narrow AI.

✅ Most AI today is Narrow AI.

Easy Trick to Remember
Narrow = One Task
Narrow AI → One specific task

2. General AI (AGI)

General AI is an AI that can think, learn, and solve different kinds of problems just like a human, instead of being limited to one specific task.

Real-Time Example (Hypothetical): Imagine an AI named Alex. You ask it to: Teach mathematics, Write software code, Drive a car, Diagnose diseases, Cook a recipe, Translate languages, Manage a company. It can perform all of these tasks with human-like understanding and adapt to new situations. ➡️ This is General AI (AGI).

Important: AGI does not exist yet. Current AI systems, including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, and DeepSeek, are not AGI. ❌ Not yet available.

Easy Trick to Remember
General = Many Tasks
General AI (AGI) → Many tasks like a human

3. Super AI

Super AI is an AI that is smarter than the smartest human and can perform every task better than humans.

Example: Movies like I, Robot. ❌ Still theoretical.

AI Categories

  • Reactive Machines: No memory. (Example: Chess AI.)
  • Limited Memory AI: Uses past information. (Example: Self-driving cars.)
  • Theory of Mind AI: Can understand emotions. (Not available yet.)
  • Self-Aware AI: AI with consciousness. (Still science fiction.)

Uses of AI

Healthcare, Banking, Education, Agriculture, Robotics, Cybersecurity, Finance, Law, Marketing, Software Development.

Advantages & Limitations

Advantages

  • Fast
  • Accurate
  • Works 24/7
  • Reduces human effort
  • Automates repetitive tasks

Limitations

  • Needs lots of data
  • Can make mistakes
  • No emotions
  • Depends on humans
  • Can be biased

📖 Lesson 2: Machine Learning Basics

Definition

Machine Learning (ML) is a branch of Artificial Intelligence (AI) that enables computers to learn from data and improve their performance without being explicitly programmed for every task.

Simple Definition

ML teaches computers to learn from experience.

Thousands of spam emails
↓
ML learns patterns
↓
New email
↓
Spam / Not Spam

Types of Machine Learning

1. Supervised Learning

Supervised Learning is a type of Machine Learning in which the model learns from labeled data, where both the input data and the correct output (target) are already known.

Labeled Data (Input + Correct Output)
↓
Train ML Model
↓
Model Learns Patterns
↓
Predict New Output
House Size Price
1000 sq.ft ₹20 Lakhs
1200 sq.ft ₹25 Lakhs
1500 sq.ft ₹30 Lakhs

Algorithms Used:

  • Linear Regression: Used to predict continuous numerical values.
  • Logistic Regression: Used for classification problems where output belongs to predefined categories.
  • Decision Tree: Makes predictions by splitting data into branches based on decision rules.
  • Random Forest: Combines multiple decision trees and uses majority voting.
  • Support Vector Machine (SVM): Separates different classes by finding the best possible boundary.

2. Unsupervised Learning

Unsupervised Learning is a type of Machine Learning in which the model learns from unlabeled data and discovers hidden patterns or groups without knowing the correct outputs.

Algorithms Used: K-Means Clustering, DBSCAN, Hierarchical Clustering.

3. Reinforcement Learning

Reinforcement Learning is a type of Machine Learning in which an agent learns by interacting with an environment and improves its behavior through rewards and penalties.

Example: Self-Driving Car (Stops at a red light → ✅ Reward | Crosses a red light → ❌ Penalty)

Interview Summary Table

Learning Type Data Used Goal Example

Unsupervised Learning Unlabeled Data Find hidden patterns or groups Customer Segmentation
Reinforcement Learning Reward & Penalty Feedback Learn the best actions through interaction Self-Driving Cars, Game AI

Deep Learning is inspired by the human brain and is widely used in Artificial Intelligence applications such as image recognition, speech recognition, self-driving cars, and AI chatbots.

📖 Lesson 4: Neural Networks

Definition

A Neural Network (Artificial Neural Network - ANN) is a computer model inspired by the human brain that learns patterns from data using interconnected artificial neurons to make predictions and decisions.

It consists of connected artificial neurons.

Structure

Input Layer
↓
Hidden Layer(s)
↓
Output Layer

Example

Input:
Dog Image
↓
Hidden Layers Detect
• Eyes
• Nose
• Legs
• Tail
↓
Output:
Dog

Structure of a Neural Network

A neural network usually has three types of layers:

Input Layer
↓
Hidden Layer(s)
↓
Output Layer

1. Input Layer

Receives the input data.
Example: Image pixels, text, numbers.

2. Hidden Layer(s)

Processes the information.
Learns patterns and relationships in the data.
A network can have one or many hidden layers.

3. Output Layer

Produces the final prediction or result.

📖 Types of Neural Networks

1. Feed Forward Neural Network (FNN)

Definition

A Feed Forward Neural Network (FNN) is the simplest type of neural network in which data flows in one direction—from the input layer, through the hidden layer(s), to the output layer—without any feedback loops.

2. Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)

Definition

A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) is a type of deep neural network specifically designed to recognize and analyze images by automatically detecting features such as edges, shapes, and objects.

3. Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)

Definition

A Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) is a type of neural network designed to process sequential data by remembering information from previous inputs, making it suitable for tasks involving time or ordered sequences.

4. Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)

Definition

Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) is an advanced type of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) that can remember important information for long periods while forgetting unnecessary information, making it effective for long sequences of data.

5. Transformer Networks

Definition

Transformer Networks are advanced deep learning models that process entire sequences in parallel using a self-attention mechanism, enabling them to understand relationships between words or data efficiently. They are the foundation of modern Large Language Models (LLMs) such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.

📖 Applications of Neural Networks

1. Image Recognition

Definition

Image Recognition is the application of neural networks to identify, classify, and recognize objects, people, or scenes in digital images.

2. Voice Recognition

Definition

Voice Recognition is the application of neural networks that enables computers to recognize, understand, and convert human speech into text or commands.

3. AI Chatbots

Definition

AI Chatbots are intelligent software applications powered by neural networks and Large Language Models (LLMs) that can understand, process, and generate human-like conversations.

4. Medical Diagnosis

Definition

Medical Diagnosis is the application of neural networks to analyze medical data, images, and patient records to help detect diseases, predict health conditions, and support doctors in making accurate diagnoses.

📖 Lesson 6: Transformers Explained

Definition

A Transformer is a deep learning model architecture that understands the relationships between words in a sentence using a mechanism called Attention, allowing it to process text efficiently and accurately.

Why Transformers?

The model transforms input text into meaningful numerical representations and then transforms those representations into useful outputs, such as translations, summaries, or answers.

Transformers process many words together using attention, making them faster and better at understanding context.

Key Components

  • 🔹 Input Tokens
  • 🔹 Embeddings
  • 🔹 Positional Encoding
  • 🔹 Self-Attention
  • 🔹 Feed Forward Layers

Real Examples

  • 💬 ChatGPT
  • 🤖 Claude
  • ✨ Gemini
  • 🚀 DeepSeek

Why Were Transformers Introduced?

Before Transformers, AI models mainly used:

  • Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN)
  • Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)

These models processed words one by one, making them slower and making it harder to remember information from long sentences.

Transformers solve these problems by:

  • ✅ Processing all words at the same time (Parallel Processing)
  • ✅ Understanding long-distance relationships between words
  • ✅ Training much faster
  • ✅ Scaling to very large datasets

Key Components of a Transformer

1. Tokenization

Splits text into smaller units (tokens).

Example:

"I love AI"
↓
I | love | AI

2. Embeddings

Converts each token into numerical vectors that represent meaning.

3. Positional Encoding

Adds information about the position of each token because Transformers process tokens in parallel.

4. Self-Attention ⭐ (Most Important)

Self-attention helps the model determine which words are most important when understanding a sentence.

Example:

"The cat chased the mouse because it was hungry."

"it" → refers to "the cat"

5. Feed Forward Neural Network

Processes the information further to produce the final output.

Characteristics of Transformers

  • ✅ Understands context very well
  • ✅ Processes all words simultaneously
  • ✅ Learns long-range relationships
  • ✅ Faster training than RNNs/LSTMs
  • ✅ Foundation of modern Large Language Models (LLMs)

📖 Lesson 7: Large Language Models (LLMs)

Definition

Large Language Models (LLMs) are advanced Artificial Intelligence (AI) models trained on massive amounts of text data to understand, generate, and respond to human language.

Why is it Called a "Large Language Model"?

It has three parts:

  • Large → Trained on huge amounts of data (books, websites, articles, code, research papers, etc.).
  • Language → Works with human languages such as English, Telugu, Hindi, French, and more.
  • Model → A trained AI system that learns patterns from data and makes predictions.

Therefore:

Large Language Model (LLM) = A large AI model trained on massive amounts of human language data.

📖 Lesson 8: Tokens & Tokenization

Definition

A Token is the smallest unit of text that an AI model processes. A token can be a word, part of a word, a number, a punctuation mark, or a symbol.

🔹 What is Tokenization?

Simple Definition

Tokenization is the process of breaking text into smaller units called tokens.

Example for Token

Sentence

I love Artificial Intelligence.

The AI may split it into tokens like:

  • I
  • love
  • Artificial
  • Intelligence
  • .

Here:

  • "I" → Token
  • "love" → Token
  • "Artificial" → Token
  • "Intelligence" → Token
  • "." → Token
Total = 5 Tokens (Simplified Example)

How Tokenization Works

Input Sentence
↓
Tokenization
↓
Tokens
↓
Embeddings
↓
AI Model (LLM)
↓
Response

Real-Time Example

User Prompt

"What is Artificial Intelligence?"

After Tokenization (Simplified):

  • What
  • is
  • Artificial
  • Intelligence
  • ?

These tokens are then converted into numerical vectors (Embeddings) before being processed by the AI model.

Why are Tokens Important?

  • ✅ AI models understand tokens, not raw sentences.
  • ✅ Context window size is measured in tokens.
  • ✅ API usage and pricing are often based on the number of tokens.
  • ✅ Longer prompts use more tokens.

📖 Lesson 9: Context Window

Definition

A Context Window is the maximum amount of information (measured in tokens) that an AI model can remember and process at one time while generating a response.

Example

ChatGPT Conversation

Imagine you're chatting with ChatGPT.

👤 You: Who is Elon Musk?

🤖 ChatGPT: Explains about Elon Musk.

👤 You: Where was he born?

ChatGPT understands that "he" refers to Elon Musk because the earlier conversation is still inside the context window.

How Context Window Works

User Prompt
↓
Conversation History
↓
Documents / Files
↓
Total Tokens
↓
Context Window
↓
LLM Generates Response

Why is Context Window Important?

A larger context window allows the AI to:

  • ✅ Remember more of the conversation.
  • ✅ Understand long documents.
  • ✅ Write large programs.
  • ✅ Summarize lengthy reports.
  • ✅ Answer questions with better context.

Characteristics of Context Window

  • ✅ Measured in tokens, not words.
  • ✅ Includes both input tokens and output tokens.
  • ✅ Larger context windows improve long conversations.
  • ✅ If the limit is exceeded, older information may no longer be considered.

📖 Lesson 10: Embeddings

Definition

Embeddings convert text into numerical vectors that capture meaning, so similar ideas are placed close together.

Embeddings are numbers that represent the meaning of words or sentences. They help AI understand that words with similar meanings are related, even if they are different words.

Example 1

Words like:

  • Dog
  • Puppy

will have similar embeddings.

Words like:

  • Dog
  • Airplane

will be much farther apart.

Example 2

Suppose we have these words:

  • Dog
  • Puppy
  • Cat
  • Car

The embedding model converts them into vectors (Simplified):

Dog → [0.21, 0.45, 0.67]
Puppy → [0.22, 0.44, 0.66]
Cat → [0.20, 0.48, 0.70]
Car → [0.91, 0.15, 0.08]

Notice:

  • Dog and Puppy have very similar vectors because their meanings are similar.
  • Car has a very different vector because its meaning is unrelated.
➡️ This is how AI understands meaning, not just exact words.

How Embeddings Work

Text
↓
Tokenization
↓
Embedding Model
↓
Numerical Vectors
↓
AI Model
↓
Response

Uses of Embeddings

  • 🔍 Semantic Search
  • 📚 RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
  • 🎯 Recommendation Systems
  • 📊 Clustering
  • 📄 Duplicate Detection

📖 Lesson 11: Hallucinations

Definition

A hallucination happens when an AI generates information that sounds correct but is false, made up, or unsupported.

Hallucination happens when an AI gives a wrong answer with confidence because it does not know the correct information or misunderstands the question.

Why Do Hallucinations Happen?

AI does not "know" facts the way humans do.

Instead, it predicts the most likely next token based on patterns it learned during training.

If the correct information is unavailable or the prompt is unclear, it may generate a convincing but incorrect answer.

How Hallucination Happens

User Question
↓
LLM Predicts Next Tokens
↓
Insufficient Knowledge or Context
↓
Incorrect / Fabricated Information
↓
Hallucination

Example

Question:

Who won the 2035 Cricket World Cup?

If the event has not happened yet, an AI might invent a winner instead of saying it does not know.

Easy Trick to Remember

Memory Formula

Wrong Prediction
↓
False Information
↓
Hallucination

📌 Interview Tip

Q: What is the difference between an AI Error and an AI Hallucination?

Answer:

AI Error: A mistake caused by incorrect input, software bugs, or poor-quality data.

AI Hallucination: A specific type of AI error where the model creates false or unsupported information but presents it confidently as if it were true.

Easy Formula

💡 Prompt Engineer Tip

To reduce hallucinations:

  • ✅ Give clear, specific instructions.
  • ✅ Provide relevant context or reference documents.
  • ✅ Use RAG for knowledge-intensive applications.
  • ✅ Ask the model to indicate uncertainty when appropriate.
  • ✅ Verify critical outputs before using them in medical, legal, financial, or other high-stakes situations.

📖 Lesson 12: Temperature

Definition

Temperature is a parameter in a Large Language Model (LLM) that controls how random, creative, or predictable the AI's responses are.

Temperature controls how creative or predictable an AI's responses are.

Temperature Values

Temperature Behavior
0.0 Very consistent and factual
0.2 Mostly factual
0.7 Balanced
1.0 More creative
1.5+ Very creative but less reliable

Why is Temperature Important?

When an AI generates text, it predicts the next token.

Sometimes there are many possible next tokens.

Temperature decides whether the AI should:

  • Choose the most likely next token.
  • Explore less likely tokens to generate more creative responses.

How Temperature Works

User Prompt
↓
LLM Predicts Many Possible Words
↓
Temperature Controls Randomness
↓
Final Response

Real-Life Analogy

Imagine 100 students taking the same exam.

👨‍🏫 Strict Teacher (Low Temperature)

Every student writes almost the same correct answer.

➜ Responses are consistent and accurate.

Uses

  • 💻 Coding → Low Temperature
  • ⚖️ Legal Writing → Low Temperature
  • 📝 Poetry → High Temperature
  • 📖 Story Writing → High Temperature

💡 Prompt Engineer Tip

  • 0.0 – 0.2 → Coding, Mathematics, Legal, Financial, Factual Answers
  • 0.5 – 0.7 → General Conversations, Emails, Documentation
  • 0.8 – 1.0 → Brainstorming, Marketing Copy, Storytelling, Creative Writing
Remember:
Lower Temperature = Higher Accuracy
Higher Temperature = Higher Creativity

📖 Lesson 13: Top-P

Definition

Top-P, also known as Nucleus Sampling, is a text generation parameter that limits token selection to the smallest set of high-probability tokens whose cumulative probability reaches the specified threshold, improving the balance between diversity and response quality.

Simple Idea

Instead of considering every possible next word, the model focuses only on the most likely group of words.

How Top-P Works

User Prompt
↓
LLM Predicts Many Possible Words
↓
Top-P Selects Most Probable Group
↓
AI Chooses Next Word
↓
Response

Real-Time Example

Prompt:

Artificial Intelligence is...
Word Probability
changing 40%
transforming 30%
improving 20%
cooking 5%
dancing 3%
sleeping 2%

Top-P = 0.90

The AI selects words until the cumulative probability reaches 90%.

Selected Word Probability Cumulative
changing 40% 40%
transforming 30% 70%
improving 20% 90%
AI chooses only from:

✅ changing
✅ transforming
✅ improving

Ignores:
❌ cooking
❌ dancing
❌ sleeping

📖 Lesson 14: Top-K

Definition

Top-K tells the AI to choose the next word only from a fixed number (K) of the most likely words, instead of considering every possible word.

Top-K limits the model to selecting from the K most likely next words.

Example

Top-K = 5

The model only considers the 5 most probable next words.

Top-P vs Top-K

Top-P Top-K
Dynamic number of choices Fixed number of choices
Adapts to probability distribution Always uses exactly K candidates
Common in modern LLMs Simpler sampling strategy

How Top-K Works

User Prompt
↓
LLM Predicts Many Possible Words
↓
Top-K Keeps Only K Highest Probability Words
↓
AI Selects One Word
↓
Response

📖 Lesson 15: AI Limitations

Definition

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is powerful, but it has several limitations. AI learns from data and patterns rather than thinking like humans.

AI cannot:

  • ❌ Think like humans.
  • ❌ Feel emotions.
  • ❌ Always know the latest information.
  • ❌ Guarantee perfect accuracy.
  • ❌ Replace human judgment in critical decisions.

Real Example

An AI may write code very quickly, but it can still introduce bugs or security issues that require human review before deployment.

Real-Life Analogy

Imagine a company receives 1,000 job applications.

Instead of interviewing all 1,000 applicants, the company first selects the Top 10 candidates based on their qualifications.

Then it chooses one person from those Top 10.

🧠 This is exactly how Top-K works.

✅ Low K → Fewer choices → More accurate and focused.
✅ High K → More choices → More creative and diverse.

📖 Lesson 17: AI Ethics

Definition

AI Ethics is the set of moral principles and guidelines that ensure Artificial Intelligence (AI) is designed, developed, and used in a fair, safe, transparent, and responsible manner.

AI Ethics is the study of moral principles for creating and using AI responsibly.

Ethical Questions

  • ❓ Is the AI fair?
  • 🔒 Is user data protected?
  • 🔍 Can people understand important AI decisions?
  • ⚖️ Who is responsible if the AI makes a harmful mistake?

Why is AI Ethics Important?

AI is increasingly used in:

  • 🏥 Healthcare
  • 🏦 Banking
  • ⚖️ Law
  • Education
  • 🚗 Self-Driving Cars
  • 💼 Hiring
  • 🛒 E-commerce

If AI is unfair, biased, or unsafe, it can negatively affect many people's lives.

➜ AI Ethics helps ensure AI is developed and used responsibly, fairly, and safely.

Core Principles of AI Ethics

1. Fairness

AI should treat everyone equally.

Example:

A recruitment AI should evaluate candidates based on their skills and qualifications, not unfairly favor or disadvantage people because of gender, race, or age.

2. Transparency

AI decisions should be understandable.

Example:

If a bank's AI rejects a loan application, it should clearly explain the reasons for that decision.

3. Accountability

Humans and organizations remain responsible for AI systems and their outcomes.

Example:

If an AI system causes harm, the company that developed or deployed it must address the issue.

4. Privacy

AI should protect users' personal information.

Example:

A healthcare AI should keep patient medical records confidential and secure.

5. Safety

AI should be reliable and should not create unnecessary risks.

Example:

A self-driving car should be thoroughly tested before operating on public roads.

6. Human Oversight

Humans should supervise important AI decisions, especially in high-risk situations.

Example:

A doctor should review an AI's medical recommendation before making the final diagnosis.

Real Example

A hiring AI should evaluate applicants based on their skills and qualifications—not unfairly favor or disadvantage groups of people.

↓

↓

Real-Life Analogy

Imagine a doctor.

A doctor has the knowledge to treat patients, but must also follow medical ethics:

  • ✅ Treat every patient fairly.
  • ✅ Keep patient information private.
  • ✅ Do no harm.
  • ✅ Act responsibly.

Similarly, AI Ethics ensures AI is developed and used in a way that benefits people while minimizing harm.

📌 Interview Tip

Q: What is the difference between AI Ethics and Responsible AI?

Answer:

AI Ethics defines the moral principles that AI should follow.

Responsible AI is the practical implementation of those principles throughout the AI lifecycle.

💡 Prompt Engineer Tip

When building AI applications such as chatbots, legal assistants, healthcare systems, or recommendation engines:

  • ✅ Avoid biased prompts and datasets.
  • ✅ Protect user privacy.
  • ✅ Be transparent about AI limitations.
  • ✅ Verify important information.
  • ✅ Keep humans involved in high-stakes decisions.
These practices help create AI systems that are both ethical and trustworthy.
